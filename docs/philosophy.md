# Philosophy: From Semantic Recovery to Reflective Commitment

Intent Commit begins from a simple observation: ordinary digital conversation often collapses several distinct acts into one gesture. A person forms a partial thought, types a sentence, and presses Send. The interface treats the typed sentence as if it were already the person's settled public utterance.

This project inserts a reflective layer between drafting and public commitment.

## A working philosophical thesis

Successful communication cannot be reduced to recovering the conventional semantic content of an expression. Hearers normally try to understand what a speaker is doing with those words: asserting, questioning, qualifying, objecting, requesting, conceding, warning, and so on.

Intent Commit adds a further claim: the relevant communicative intention is not always fully formed before language appears. A speaker may discover, revise, or delimit what they are prepared to mean while inspecting a structured interpretation of their own draft.

For that reason, the system does not model the agent as a machine that "extracts the speaker's true intention." It models the agent as a fallible reflective instrument.

## Minimal model

```text
initial expression
      ↓
agent interpretation
      ↓
speaker reflection
      ↓
clarification / revision
      ↓
reflectively endorsed intention
      ↓
committed utterance
```

The key object is therefore not merely a pre-existing intention, but a **reflectively endorsed communicative commitment**.

## Normative consequence

AI output does not acquire public authority merely because it is fluent. Public authority remains with the human speaker. The human decides which formulation becomes attributable to them.

This produces a deliberate asymmetry:

- AI may propose interpretations.
- AI may expose ambiguity.
- AI may ask for missing reasons.
- AI may suggest a faithful reformulation.
- Only the speaker may commit the utterance.

## Why "Commit"

The word is intentionally borrowed from software practice. A working copy can be tentative and revisable; a commit marks a version one is prepared to enter into shared history. Intent Commit applies the same distinction to conversation.
